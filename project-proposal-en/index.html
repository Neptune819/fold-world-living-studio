03Inspiration & References1:00–2:10

Painting, storytelling,
space and camera.

I use references to guide four parts of the animation: its painted appearance, the order in which the story unfolds, the design of its environments, and the way each shot presents an event.

01 / ART STYLE

Retro science fiction and a painted surface

I reference a hand-painted oil style: broad brushstrokes, distinct colour masses, simplified detail and edges that soften into the atmosphere. Muted blue-grey, cream and ochre guide the colour direction. People, buildings and machinery should feel part of the same painted image, with texture remaining stable during movement.

John Harris is a composition reference named in my storyboard material. I am interested in monumental scale, atmospheric depth and the relationship between small figures and large structures.

02 / NARRATIVE APPROACH

Curiosity, clear causality and gradual discovery

Made in Abyss inspires my interest in a setting with a sense of history, mystery and discovery. I want viewers to feel that a world extends beyond what a single shot reveals.

For storytelling, I reference gradual world-building and clear cause-and-effect relationships. Establishing an atmosphere before introducing change helps viewers follow an unfamiliar setting. Images, pacing and narration should work together, leaving room for curiosity without making the sequence difficult to understand.

03 / ENVIRONMENT DESIGN

Retrofuturist and industrial visual languages

I draw on retrofuturist architecture for imaginative silhouettes, large spatial relationships and the combination of technology with recognisable human spaces. I also reference dieselpunk and industrial design for material weight, exposed pipes, mechanical structures and dense detail.

Lighting, palette, spatial openness and surface texture help distinguish moods. Soft ambient light can make a space feel calm and expansive; local light, stronger shadows and compressed framing can make it feel heavier or more uncertain.

04 / CAMERA DESIGN

Readable space and purposeful movement

Wide shots and measured camera movement establish scale and orientation. Slow pushes, lateral moves and pull-backs give viewers time to read a scene. Low angles can emphasise size, while fixed views can make a specific action easier to follow.

I treat movement as a storytelling choice: each shot needs a clear subject, a reason to move and enough time for the viewer to understand it. Changes in shot size and pace can direct attention and build tension while preserving spatial continuity.

Painted retro science fiction; gradual discovery and clear causality; retrofuturist and industrial design references; camera movement that guides attention and makes space readable.

05Tools, Production & AI Roles3:50–4:50

From a script
to an edited sequence.

I use Seedance 2.5 / minmaxh3 for video generation and Jianying / DaVinci Resolve for post-production. AI produces visual and motion candidates; I decide their narrative purpose and final use.

Story Script→Storyboard→Reference Images→Video Generation→Voice-over & Editing

Story script

Input: my background story. Output: the events selected for the animation. I decide what viewers need to understand and what can remain implicit, checking that the selected events connect clearly.

Storyboard

I turn the selected text into shots, using AI assistance to develop descriptions and reviewing them myself. Each entry defines the setting, subject, action, camera and narration. The output is a shot plan; I check that each shot communicates one main event.

Reference images

I generate images from the shot descriptions to establish composition, appearance and environment. I select suitable references and revise unclear results. These become the basis for motion generation.

Video generation

I combine references with motion instructions in Seedance 2.5 / minmaxh3. The output is a set of candidate clips. I compare action, subject stability, painted texture and continuity, then retain or regenerate versions according to the storyboard.

Voice-over & editing

Selected clips and narration enter the editing timeline. I arrange voice-over and use Jianying / DaVinci Resolve to control order, timing and post-production. The output should be a coherent background film; I check whether images and narration support each other.

06Questions & Expected Challenges4:50–5:40

Keep the images connected.
Keep the music flowing.

My main challenges are visual continuity and musical continuity: separately produced shots and music need to work together as one coherent animation.

Visual continuity

Generated clips can differ in character appearance, architecture, colour, lighting and painted texture. Even when individual shots look convincing, these differences can become noticeable at a cut.

I will use shared references and compare adjacent shots to keep the visual style consistent. I also need to check movement direction, camera position and the ending and starting poses, so each transition feels connected. Where needed, I will select another version, regenerate a shot, or adjust the cut and colour in editing.

Musical continuity

The soundtrack needs a consistent musical style and emotional progression across different scenes. Abrupt changes in tempo, instrumentation or volume can make the film feel fragmented, while a single unchanged track may fail to support changes in mood.

I plan to establish a coherent musical direction, place transitions at suitable musical phrases or beats, and use fades, crossfades and volume adjustments where needed. I will review the assembled film to check that the music develops naturally with the images and leaves space for the narration.

Visual continuity: consistent appearance, painted style and movement between shots.
Musical continuity: a coherent soundtrack with smooth transitions in rhythm, mood and volume.

How can I make separately generated shots feel visually continuous?

How can the music stay connected while supporting changes in mood?
